Methodology

Stablecoin payment flows are segmented into B2B, C2C, C2B, and B2C categories using a combination of behavioral pattern analysis, entity labels, cross-referencing against external data sources, and validation through discussions with industry participants. While the classification is inherently probabilistic, the methodology is designed to provide a directional view of real-economy stablecoin usage. Each segment is identified through a distinct set of on-chain behavioral signals:

B2B

Business to Business

Larger ticket sizes, non-reversible directional transfers, low counterparty diversity, and recurring patterns consistent with invoice settlement.

Large tickets Recurring Low diversity Non-reversible

Consumer to Consumer

High transaction counts, smaller average ticket sizes, and broad counterparty diversity — peer-to-peer value transfer.

Day of Week Breakdown

A key narrative around stablecoin payments adoption is that blockchains serve as 24/7 payments rails. The data supports this: while weekday volumes are highest across all segments, weekends still capture meaningful activity — Sunday B2B volume is ~56% of Monday's peak, and C2C flows remain relatively stable throughout the week. C2B and C2C flows show the least weekend dropoff, while B2B and B2C volumes decline more significantly, suggesting consumers are more day-of-week agnostic than businesses.

This pattern is consistent with traditional payments behavior, where business-driven flows concentrate on working days, but notably the consumer-side persistence reinforces the 24/7 utility thesis for stablecoins.

Geography

v1 Methodology

We attribute each blockchain address to a region and country using three signals — deposits to local exchanges, withdrawals from local exchanges, and a timezone-based activity classifier. To identify local exchange interactions, we combine proprietary centralized exchange (CEX) labels with cross-chain transfer datasets.

The charts below show the relative regional share of payments volume sent or received over time, based on only high-confidence addresses. Note that the % of volume sent by geography includes payments where the receiver geography is uncertain, and vice versa.
