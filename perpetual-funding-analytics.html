What Are Perpetual Funding Rates?

Perpetual futures contracts have no expiry date. To keep their price aligned with spot, exchanges use a funding rate mechanism — periodic payments between long and short holders. Understanding and tracking these rates is critical for derivatives traders.

  • Positive funding — longs pay shorts (bullish crowding)
  • Negative funding — shorts pay longs (bearish crowding)
  • Extreme rates — often precede mean-reversion moves

Frequently Asked Questions

What are perpetual funding rates?

Perpetual funding rates are periodic payments exchanged between long and short traders to keep the perpetual futures price anchored to the spot price. Positive rates mean longs pay shorts; negative rates mean shorts pay longs. Tracking these rates helps identify market sentiment and yield opportunities.

What is open interest and why does it matter?

Open interest (OI) measures the total number of outstanding derivative contracts. Rising OI with rising prices signals strong trend conviction. Falling OI during price moves suggests the trend may reverse. Nexxore tracks OI across 20 markets in real-time.
